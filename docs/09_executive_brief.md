# Executive Brief — -10.7% group revenue

**Mavuno Home & Care** · Q3 2026 vs Q3 2025 · prepared 2026-10-05 · Internal — Commercial leadership

> Group net revenue fell KSh 5.21M (10.7%). We did not lose customers — active customers rose +5.5%. We lost trips (-11.4% orders per customer) and basket (-4.5% average order value), and 71% of the damage sits in two of six stores.

### 1. We did not lose customers — we lost trips and basket size

Active customers rose +5.5%, so acquisition is working. But orders per customer fell -11.4% and average order value fell -4.5%. The engine failed, not the funnel — and that single distinction changes who is accountable and what the fix is.

*So what: A funnel problem would be fixed in marketing. A frequency and basket problem is fixed in CRM, assortment and availability.*

### 2. Two of six stores carry 71% of the decline

Mombasa Nyali (-27.0%) and Thika Road (-18.7%) are not a chain-wide trend. They are two local, separately-diagnosable events inside one quarter.

*So what: Treating this as a chain-wide trend would spread a thin fix across six stores instead of a targeted fix on two.*

### 3. The largest single cost has a date, a store and an owner

Two hero Personal Care SKUs sat off the shelf at Mombasa Nyali for 76 days (9.5% shelf availability). It cost KSh 2.16M. Only KSh 0.45M of that is Personal Care revenue — KSh 1.71M is baskets that were never started.

*So what: Measuring only the affected category understates the event by roughly 4.8x. Availability failures are basket failures, not category failures.*

### 4. Discounting, not pricing, is the second lever

List price per unit rose +4.3% while discount depth went from 4.80% to 7.82% of gross — roughly an extra KSh 1.43M given away in the quarter, concentrated in Beverages (+13.05pp).

*So what: Gross profit fell faster than revenue. Price rises are being handed straight back through promotions, so margin — not just revenue — is the thing at risk.*

### 5. Retention is healthy; the leak is trip frequency

4,410 of 5,265 Q3 2025 customers returned (83.8%), and 1,145 new customers arrived. Churn is not the problem — the people we keep are simply shopping less often.

*So what: Win-back for the 855 lapsed customers is worth doing, but the money is in raising frequency among the customers who already come back.*

## What would change this conclusion

- 0.76% of revenue carries no customer identifier (KSh 0.70M of anonymous walk-ins). Every segment-level conclusion therefore reconciles to 99.24% of revenue, not 100%. The reconciliation is shown as its own line rather than hidden in an 'Other' bucket.
- The dataset is simulated from a documented mechanism (docs/00_ground_truth.md). That is deliberate: it lets the diagnosis be graded. See outputs/validation_report.md — 14/14 checks pass against the sealed answer key.
- No procurement, lead-time or supplier-performance data exists in this extract. The fifth why therefore stops at a data-capture recommendation instead of a guess.
- The annualised figure is a stated run-rate (KSh -20.83M, the quarter's change x 4), not a forecast. It assumes the Mombasa Nyali incident does not repeat and ignores Q4 seasonality.
